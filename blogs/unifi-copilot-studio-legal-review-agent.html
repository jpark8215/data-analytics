How Unifi Cut Contract Processing from Days to Minutes Using a Copilot Studio Legal Review Agent

A deep dive into how the aviation services company combined deterministic Power Automate workflows with an AI agent to extract, classify, and validate legal terms across documents.

Published: October 7, 2026

Rapid growth rarely occurs without operational friction. When Unifi Aviation Services expanded its workforce from 10,000 to over 45,000 employees, the company became North America's largest provider of aviation ground handling services. However, this massive operational scaling placed an unprecedented burden on their legal and administrative teams. Hundreds of hours were consumed annually by the manual review of complex collective bargaining agreements and high-stakes vendor contracts.

Previous attempts to implement off-the-shelf AI solutions had failed to meet expectations, largely because aviation contracts feature highly specialized industry terminology and nuanced legal structures. To solve this bottleneck, Unifi took a different approach. They architected a custom legal review agent using Microsoft Copilot Studio and the Power Platform. The resulting system successfully compressed contract processing times from several days down to a matter of minutes.

The Problem with Monolithic AI Agents

When organizations first experiment with generative AI for document processing, the instinct is often to upload a massive PDF into a conversational agent and ask it to "extract all the important terms." This monolithic approach frequently results in hallucinations, missed clauses, and processing timeouts. Generative models are exceptional at reasoning and summarization, but they can be unpredictable when tasked with rigid, large-scale data extraction.

Unifi recognized this architectural limitation early in their design process. Instead of forcing a single Copilot Studio agent to handle the entire lifecycle of a contract, they deliberately decoupled the workflow. They separated the deterministic tasks of document ingestion and data extraction from the non-deterministic tasks of semantic reasoning and natural language querying.

The Decoupled Architecture Advantage:

By splitting the workload, Unifi achieved a highly reliable system. Deterministic tools handle the rigid work of optical character recognition and baseline text extraction. The AI agent is then deployed specifically for what it does best: interpreting legal nuance, answering complex conversational queries, and summarizing dense clauses based on the already-extracted text.

Combining Deterministic Workflows with Generative AI

The solution Unifi deployed relies heavily on the synergy between Power Automate and Copilot Studio. The process begins not with a chat prompt, but with a deterministic workflow.

Step 1: Ingestion and Extraction via Power Automate

When a new contract is received, a Power Automate flow triggers automatically. This flow handles the heavy lifting of document ingestion. It utilizes intelligent document processing capabilities to scan the file, recognize the structure of the collective bargaining agreement, and extract the raw text and standard clauses with high fidelity. Because Power Automate is deterministic, this step executes predictably every single time, ensuring that no pages are skipped and no baseline data is lost in an AI hallucination.

Step 2: Structuring the Data

Once the raw text is extracted, the workflow structures this information into a searchable database format. The contract is no longer an impenetrable PDF; it is a structured dataset containing specific clauses, effective dates, and localized aviation terms. This structured repository becomes the grounded knowledge base for the AI agent.

Step 3: Reasoning and Querying via Copilot Studio

With the data safely extracted and structured, the Copilot Studio agent takes over the user interface layer. Legal professionals interact with the agent using natural language. They can ask complex questions such as, "What are the specific overtime provisions for ground handlers in the newly acquired terminal?"

Because the agent is grounded strictly in the structured data provided by the Power Automate workflow, its responses are highly accurate and directly cited. The legal team is not waiting for the AI to read a 200-page document from scratch; they are querying an agent that sits on top of a pre-processed, optimized database.

Evaluating the Impact and Trade-Offs

The operational results of this architecture have been transformative for Unifi. The most immediate benefit is velocity. Tasks that historically required a legal analyst to spend days reading through comparative contract language are now completed in minutes. This acceleration allows the legal department to scale its support alongside the company's rapid geographic expansion without requiring a proportional increase in headcount.

Furthermore, the custom Copilot Studio solution proved to be significantly more cost-effective than purchasing specialized, vendor-locked legal tech platforms. By building on their existing Microsoft infrastructure, Unifi maintained control over their data governance and integration points.

However, this multi-layered approach does introduce specific trade-offs. Building a decoupled system requires more upfront engineering effort than simply pointing a basic chatbot at a SharePoint folder. Maintaining the integration between Power Automate's extraction flows and Copilot Studio's knowledge base requires continuous monitoring. If the structure of incoming contracts changes drastically, the deterministic extraction flows may require manual recalibration to ensure the AI agent continues to receive properly structured data.

Conclusion

The Unifi case study provides a compelling blueprint for enterprise AI adoption. It demonstrates that the most effective AI implementations are rarely single, monolithic bots. By deliberately combining the rigid reliability of Power Automate with the flexible reasoning of Copilot Studio, Unifi created a robust legal review system. They shifted the burden of manual document processing to software, freeing their legal professionals to focus entirely on high-value strategy and judgment. This architectural choice to separate extraction from reasoning is what ultimately allowed them to cut processing times from days to minutes.
